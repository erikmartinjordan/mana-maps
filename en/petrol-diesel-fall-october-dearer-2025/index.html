Petrol and diesel fall in October but remain dearer than in 2025

Petrol is 10% cheaper in October at €1.75 a litre and diesel sits at €1.88. The 20-cent subsidy will stay in November, but filling a tank now costs between €6 and €16 more than a year ago.

Newsroom · 30 October 2026 · 6 min read

Fuel prices are heading into the Todos los Santos long weekend after four consecutive weeks of falls. Petrol stands at €1.75 a litre, 10% less than at the start of October, and diesel at €1.88. The relief is real but short-lived: according to the EU Oil Bulletin, filling a 50-litre tank now costs €87.50 for petrol and €94 for diesel, against €81.51 and €77.60 a year ago.

What happened

October's fall does not erase the past year's rise. Diesel inflation reached 34% year on year in September and petrol 27.5%, according to INE. Those components pushed transport to 10.5% and lifted headline CPI by six tenths to 4.9%, its highest level since February 2023. Core inflation stood at 3.1%.

The government is therefore keeping the 20-cent-per-litre rebate in November. The mechanism keeps it at its maximum while annual fuel inflation exceeds 15%; with diesel at 34% and petrol at 27.5%, that condition is met. Electricity does not qualify: its annual rate was 11.4%, so there will be no VAT or electricity-tax cuts, according to the economy ministry.

Without the aid, the Spanish Confederation of Service Station Employers estimates petrol would cost €1.99 a litre and diesel €2.14, above the two-euro mark. With it, both remain below that line but dearer than in 2025: about €6 more per tank for petrol and €16.40 more for diesel. The DGT expects 5.3 million long-distance journeys this weekend.

The energy backdrop still dominates. The war in Iran keeps pressure on crude and wholesale electricity is hitting annual highs: €244.69 per megawatt-hour on Friday 30, according to OMIE. The harmonised HICP rate has doubled since February, from 2.5% to 5% in September, 1.2 points above the EU average.

The map: the starting point, not today's figure

The map brings together annual inflation for 187 countries and territories using the latest World Bank data, updated in October 2026. It is not a snapshot of September: for Spain, the value shown is 2.67% for 2025. It sets the starting point and compares Spain's position with its neighbours; it does not read today's 4.9%.

At the end of 2025, Spain (2.67%) was above France (0.94%), Italy (1.53%), Germany (2.17%), Portugal (2.34%) and Belgium (2.47%), and below the United Kingdom (3.88%). The euro-area average is not a separate feature; comparisons have to be country by country. The jump to 4.9% is above all a recent, energy-driven phenomenon.

The map also orders the rest of the world. Venezuela holds the highest figure, 254.95% in 2016; Argentina recorded 219.88% in 2024 and Turkey 34.88% in 2025. China stood at 0.06%. These figures are not comparable without checking the year: the map uses each country's latest available data.

What comes next

INE publishes the October flash CPI estimate this Friday, the first pointer to whether the 4.9% rate has peaked. The final figure is due on 13 November. The 20-cent subsidy will stay in November; the decree cuts it to 13 cents in the first month fuel inflation falls below 15% and to six cents the following month.

The Todos los Santos traffic operation starts at 15:00 on Friday and runs until midnight on Sunday 1 in most regions, and until Monday 2 in eight autonomous communities. The DGT expects 5.3 million long-distance journeys. With fuel inflation still at 27.5% for petrol and 34% for diesel, the long weekend will also test household budgets.

The underlying question is not whether fuel falls for a week, but how long it takes to return to 2025 prices. The map shows that Spanish inflation was already moderate before the energy shock; today's 4.9% is the exception.
