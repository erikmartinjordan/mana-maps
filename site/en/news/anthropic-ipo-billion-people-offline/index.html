Anthropic's IPO and the 2.155 billion people still offline

The maker of Claude prices its shares on 18 November at a valuation of up to $1.4tn, with revenue running at $81bn a year. We cross that figure with the map of internet users by country: 2.155 billion people are still offline, and there is no customer there.

Newsroom · 15 November 2026 · 6 min read

Anthropic will sell 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, according to the amended filing reported by CNBC. At the top of the range the offering would raise $64.35bn and value the company at $1.405tn on a fully diluted basis, up from the $965bn it announced in May. The price is set on Wednesday 18 November and the shares start trading on Thursday 19 November on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

Demand is far ahead of supply: orders amounted to about six times the shares on offer, according to Bloomberg. The offering comes with a $26bn private placement of Class N shares bought by Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD and Micron.

The filing explains both the interest and the doubt: $33.24bn of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 (up 1,206%) and about $81bn annualised at the end of October, against purchase commitments of $895.4bn, mostly for compute, of which $69.8bn falls due within twelve months.

The sum, step by step

To size the floor of that market we use two World Bank series downloaded on 15 November 2026 from api.worldbank.org: individuals using the internet (IT.NET.USER.ZS) and total population (SP.POP.TOTL), both for 2024. We match the 182 countries that hold both values, out of the 217 economies the institution publishes, and add population weighted by penetration: people online = Σ(population × percentage ÷ 100). Regional aggregates are excluded.

The result covers 7.883 billion people, almost the world's population. Of those, 5.729 billion used the internet (72.7%); the remaining 2.155 billion did not.

The customer who is not there

The shortfall is not spread evenly. Of the 182 countries analysed, 55 are above 90% penetration and hold 2.656 billion people, a third of the sample (33.7%). These are markets where being online is already the norm: the United States (94.7%), Germany (93.5%) and Spain (95.8%) on the map.

The other end concentrates the gap. India leads with 508.6 million people offline, 23.6% of the world total. With Nigeria, China, Pakistan, Ethiopia, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Bangladesh and Indonesia it adds up to 1.214 billion of the 2.155 billion: eight countries holding 56.4%. Three are in sub-Saharan Africa and three more in South Asia.

$14.10 per connected person

If the $81bn annualised revenue were spread across everyone online, each would contribute about $14.10 a year. That is not how the business works — Anthropic mostly sells to other companies, according to CNBC — but it does mark its scale: $14 a year per connected person only adds up if the customer is a company with a large contract. The company itself lists a narrow customer base among its risk factors.

Read from the other side of the map, the same sum says where it is easier to charge: the 55 countries above 90% penetration hold 2.656 billion people, so all revenue coming from there would be $30.50 per head per year. Little for an enterprise product: the business rests on a handful of large corporate contracts.

The limits of the arithmetic

Three caveats. The first is methodological: the map colours each country with its latest available year — 2024 in 171 of its 205 polygons — while the calculation uses 2024 only so the series are comparable; that is why India reads 70.0% for 2025 on the map and 64.9% in the table. The second: connectivity is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one; it does not measure AI adoption or ability to pay. The third: a valuation is not revenue. The $1.4tn measures what the market expects, not what the company earns today.

With those caveats, the map puts a floor under a conversation that usually floats in the air. On Thursday 19 November we will know the price at which Anthropic crosses; the 2.155 billion people offline will still be there the next day.
