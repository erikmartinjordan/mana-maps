Argentina, Brazil and Mexico: jobs hold up, credit bites

Argentina has posted two consecutive quarterly falls and its country risk premium is above 1,000 basis points; Brazil has cut its 2026 growth forecast to 1.9%; Mexico is growing 3.3% year on year. The unemployment map shows what they share: jobs are not what is breaking.

Newsroom · 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

Three figures published this week sum up three speeds. Argentine activity fell 1.8% in the third quarter from the previous one, according to consultants' estimates based on the monthly indicator INDEC released on Tuesday; with the 0.6% fall in the second quarter, the country is in a technical recession. Brazil cut its 2026 growth forecast on Monday, from 2.0% to 1.9%. Mexico grew 3.3% year on year between July and September.

The gap between the two is more than four points on annual rates: Argentina's indicator was 1.2% lower year on year in September, while Mexico's GDP grew 3.3%. Yet all three labour markets remain tight. That is the first clue the map offers: neither Argentina's recession nor Brazil's slowdown arrives through a collapse in employment.

Three quarters, three speeds

In Argentina the monthly activity estimator rose 1.1% in September from August, but was 1.2% below September 2025, and the year-to-date gain is 1.0%. Official third-quarter GDP lands on 16 December. The financial front weighs more: country risk hit 1,121 basis points on Wednesday — against about 400 in mid-July — and the retail dollar broke through 1,600 pesos.

Brazil is not in recession, but the Finance Ministry's MacroFiscal Bulletin cut its 2026 forecast to 1.9% and its 2027 forecast to 1.8% on Monday. The document blames the cost of credit: the central bank held the Selic at 13.75% this month. The inflation forecast rises to 5.3%, above the 4.5% ceiling.

Mexico is moving the other way. GDP rose 0.9% on the quarter and 3.3% year on year in the third quarter, with services up 1.2%; January to September is running at 1.9%. Banxico cut rates by 25 basis points on 5 November, to 6.25%.

Employment is not the channel

The map puts numbers on the labour market. Mexico records 2.7% modelled unemployment in 2025; Brazil 6.0%; Argentina 7.1%. The median across the 32 Latin American and Caribbean countries and territories with a figure on the map is 6.0%: Argentina sits just above it, Brazil on it and Mexico well below. Chile (9.0%), Colombia (8.3%) and Uruguay (7.5%) all run higher than Argentina.

That does not make Argentina's labour market healthy: registered employment is showing strain, according to the diagnosis gathered this week, and the map measures open unemployment, not job quality. In Mexico the warning runs the other way. INEGI put third-quarter unemployment at 2.9%, alongside 55.0% labour informality: low unemployment is not the same as secure work.

What the map cannot say is why these economies are growing or contracting. The other figures do that job: the difference between the three countries is not how many people are looking for work, but what it costs to borrow.

What comes next

INDEC publishes third-quarter GDP on 16 December and will confirm or revise the technical recession. Brazil faces its next rate decision with forecast inflation above the ceiling. Mexico's next quarterly labour survey is due on 25 February. In the meantime the map offers a quick read: if employment holds in all three cases, the adjustment ahead will be financial, not labour-market.
